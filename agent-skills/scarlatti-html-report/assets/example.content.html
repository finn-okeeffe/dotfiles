Evaluation report

Survey completion fell after the reminder schedule changed

Moving the second reminder from day 7 to day 14 cost about nine percentage points of completion. Restoring the day 7 reminder is the simplest fix.

Prepared for Example Trust · 3 November 2026 · Draft for discussion

Key findings

  • Completion fell from 62% in 2025 to 53% in 2026.
  • The drop is concentrated among respondents who had not opened the first email.
  • Respondents who started the survey finished it at the same rate in both years.

What changed

In 2026 the second reminder moved from day 7 to day 14. Everything else, including the invitation wording and incentive, stayed the same.

Where responses were lost

Cumulative responses tracked closely until day 7, when the 2025 reminder went out.

How the effect was estimated

We compared completion in each year with a logistic model, controlling for region and device. The estimated reminder effect is the coefficient \(\beta_1\) in

$$\operatorname{logit}\Pr(\text{complete}_i) = \beta_0 + \beta_1\,\text{late}_i + \boldsymbol{\gamma}^\top \mathbf{x}_i$$

where \(\text{late}_i = 1\) for 2026 invitations. The fitted \(\hat\beta_1 = -0.37\) (95% CI \(-0.52\) to \(-0.22\)) corresponds to a fall of about nine percentage points at the 2025 baseline.

Limitation

The two years differ in more than the reminder date. Event timing and media coverage could account for part of the gap.

Recommended next steps

  1. Restore the day 7 reminder for the 2027 round.
  2. Test a third reminder at day 14 with half the sample.
  3. Report completion weekly during fieldwork so problems show up earlier.

Decision needed: approve the 2027 reminder schedule by 1 December 2026.
